Kingdom of the Baquates: Autonomy and Diplomacy in Roman-Era Volubilis

Published on November 21, 2026

Following the Roman annexation of northern Mauretania, the Kingdom of the Baquates emerged as a powerful indigenous Moroccan kingdom centered in the Middle Atlas and the plains around Volubilis.

Latin inscriptions carved on commemorative altars at Volubilis record successive 'Reges' (Kings) and 'Principes' of this Moroccan kingdom—such as Canartha, Nuffusi, Ililasene, and Septimius Severus—concluding formal peace treaties (foedera) with Roman governors.

These epigraphic records confirm the continuity of autonomous Moroccan kingship during antiquity.
