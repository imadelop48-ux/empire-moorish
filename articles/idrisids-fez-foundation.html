The Idrisid Dynasty: The First Centralized Islamic Moroccan State

Published on November 21, 2026

Emerging in the late 8th century around Volubilis (Walili), the Idrisid dynasty established the first centralized Islamic Moroccan kingdom to unify Morocco, laying enduring political, urban, and cultural foundations.

Under Idris I and his son Idris II—who expanded Fez into a thriving imperial capital—this Moroccan kingdom extended from Tangier in the Ghomara region in the north to the Chelif River in the east, and southward across the Sahara to the borders of the western Bilad al-Sudan, leaving a deeply rooted scientific and cultural radiance across Morocco and the Islamic West.

Regarding the Al-Qarawiyyin Mosque in Fez, while later literary chronicles such as Ibn Abi Zar's Rawd al-Qirtas attribute its foundation to Fatima al-Fihri in 859 CE, archaeological and epigraphic findings inside the monument attest that its construction was ordered by the Idrisid ruler Imam Dawud ibn Idris II.
