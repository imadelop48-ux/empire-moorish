Kingdom of Mauretania: From the 6th–5th Centuries BCE, Atlas, and Antaeus to Baga and Bocchus I

Published on November 21, 2026

The historical roots of the ancient Moroccan Kingdom of Mauretania extend back to the 6th and 5th centuries BCE across northwestern Morocco, an era marked by the rise of indigenous Mauri polities alongside flourishing Atlantic and Mediterranean centers such as Tingis (Tangier), Lixus, Volubilis (Walili), and Sala.

According to ancient historians, Atlas and Antaeus were regarded as early kings of this Moroccan kingdom in mythological accounts and may very well have been historical monarchs; among the most prominent surviving physical monuments of this early era is the megalithic tumulus of Mzoura (Msoura) near Larache.

Archaeological evidence from Lixus, Mogador, and the Mzoura complex reflects organized local chiefdoms and early royal authority engaged in maritime trade and monumental funerary architecture during the 6th and 5th centuries BCE.

By the late 3rd century BCE, written historical sources attest to King Baga, a sovereign monarch of this Moroccan kingdom who commanded substantial cavalry forces and shaped western Mediterranean geopolitics during the Second Punic War.

In the late 2nd and 1st centuries BCE, Bocchus I consolidated the Moroccan kingdom's borders and diplomacy, followed by Bocchus II and Bogud, who minted royal coinage in Punic and Latin scripts before the illustrious reigns of Juba II and Ptolemy (until 40 CE).
